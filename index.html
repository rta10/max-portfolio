Medical Triage Handoff Assistant App

This is a project that sits at the exact intersection of healthcare operations, data integrity, and AI-driven safety. Below you will find a link to launch the Medical Triage Handoff simulation that incorporates the information described for this concept.

The Concept:

The "Digital Scribe" for the paramedic.

  • Input: Paramedic uses voice-to-text to relay the ATMIST details during transport.
  • Processing: The AI instantly structures this into a clean, standardized "Digital Handoff Card."
  • Output: The information is presented on a tablet in the ED before the ambulance arrives, allowing the triage nurse to see a "Headlines" summary of the patient.
1. The Systematic Handover Protocol

To ensure accuracy, the industry standard is to use a structured mnemonic like ATMIST or SBAR for a "High-Reliability Handover" protocols between paramedics and Hospital Emergency Departments. As well, the “Handoff” assistant uses Military-Grade AES-256-GCM Encryption: The application now dynamically marshals patient demographics (e.g., Name, Age, Gender) and active clinical vitals into a JSON payload, immediately encrypting it with a unique Initialization Vector.

The "High-Reliability" Handover Checklist:
  • A - Age/Gender: Identify the patient clearly.
  • T - Time: Time of incident vs. time of arrival.
  • M - Mechanism/Medical History: What happened? (e.g., "Fall from height" or "Sudden onset chest pain").
  • I - Injuries/Illness: What is the primary clinical problem?
  • S - Signs: Most recent vital signs (BP, HR, SpO2) and trends (e.g., "BP dropped 20 points since last reading").
  • T - Treatment: What have you done? (e.g., "Administered 324mg Aspirin at 14:00").
  • "The Gap" (Your unique contribution): A specific statement on "Response to Treatment"—did the patient improve, stay the same, or deteriorate?

Beginner Welding Learning Kit

A source-grounded AI content synthesis project using NotebookLM to turn technical manuals and transcripts into beginner-focused reference material, troubleshooting content, an audio overview, and a visual slide deck.

Project Presentation

Beginner Welder “Cheat Sheet”

AI Tool Implemented: NotebookLM

Quick-Start Cheat Sheet

PROMPT: Based on the provided documents and transcripts, create a 'Quick-Start Cheat Sheet' for a beginner. Include a 3-column table: Welding Method, Best Use Case, Difficulty Level. Then, list the 5 most critical safety rules that appear across all sources.

The resulting cheat sheet compared MIG, Stick, and TIG welding by intended use and relative learning difficulty, then synthesized five recurring safety priorities from the provided sources: eye protection, skin protection, ventilation, fire prevention, and use of an appropriate work surface.

MIG Welding Troubleshooting Table

Next PROMPT: Create a 'Troubleshooting Table' for MIG welding. List common defects, like spatter or burn-through, what likely caused them, and the specific adjustment needed to fix them (cite your sources).

NotebookLM generated a structured troubleshooting reference covering common symptoms such as burn-through, excessive spatter, wire burn-back, lack of penetration, undercutting, porosity, poor fusion, and irregular popping or cracking sounds. Each entry connected the observed defect to likely causes and a corresponding corrective category.

Explaining “The Puddle” for a Beginner

Next PROMPT: Explain the concept of 'The Puddle' as if you were talking to a total beginner. Use an analogy to make it easy to understand, and explain why mastering this is more important than equipment settings.

The response introduced the molten weld puddle through a wax-and-heat-gun analogy, then used the source material to challenge part of the prompt itself. Rather than simply accepting the assumption that technique is always more important than equipment settings, NotebookLM reported that the provided sources emphasized correct machine settings first, followed by puddle control as the key to physical technique once the machine is configured appropriately.

Source-grounded behavior

An important part of the experiment was seeing whether the AI would preserve what the source material actually said instead of blindly following an incorrect premise embedded in the prompt.

Pushing vs. Dragging the Torch

NEXT PROMPT: Explain the difference between “pushing” and “dragging” the torch.

The generated explanation contrasted the two travel orientations in plain language and summarized why the referenced sources recommended different approaches depending on the welding position and application.

Audio Overview

To test the AI's ability to synthesize technical manuals into accessible formats, an Audio Overview was generated. The project describes this as a conversational, podcast-style translation of complex safety protocols and welding physics, demonstrating the potential for AI to act as an on-demand tutor for hands-on technical subjects.

Beginner Slide Deck

NEXT PROMPT: Create a slide deck for beginners with step by step instructions. Be sure to include safety protocols and equipment. Use images to ensure understanding of steps and equipment.

The final deliverable was a Google Slide Deck that translated the same technical source material into an easier-to-follow visual presentation, providing another example of using NotebookLM to reformat dense technical knowledge for a different learning medium.
